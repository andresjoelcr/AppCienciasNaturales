5. Proposal, Limitations and Future Works

The evidence synthesized in this review, combined with the implementation
and empirical evaluation of the developed system, supports a concrete
technological and pedagogical integration framework for AI-powered mobile
education. Figure X presents a structured overview of the proposal, its
inherent limitations, and the directions proposed for future research.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1. Proposal

Based on the findings from the systematic review and guided by the
identified research gap, this study proposes an AI-powered mobile educational
application developed with Flutter, designed to support the teaching and
learning of Natural Sciences at the secondary level (8th grade). The system
integrates three specialized AI-driven modules within a unified mobile
interface, each addressing a distinct dimension of the learning process:

(1) An Educational Chatbot powered by the LLaMA 3.3 70B model via the Groq
API, which provides conversational tutoring in Spanish. The chatbot employs
a curricular context injection strategy — embedding the full instructional
content of the didactic guide into the system prompt — to ensure scientifically
accurate, domain-restricted responses aligned with the official curriculum.
This approach enables personalized, on-demand explanatory support without
requiring costly model fine-tuning.

(2) A Dynamic Quiz Generator, also driven by LLaMA 3.3 70B via Groq API,
which produces adaptive assessments across three difficulty levels (basic,
intermediate, advanced). Quiz generation is anchored to specific subtopic
content, ensuring curricular alignment and supporting formative feedback
loops that reinforce active learning.

(3) An Intelligent Image Scanner that combines Google Gemini 1.5 Flash for
primary multimodal identification of biological specimens with a TensorFlow
Lite MobileNet V2 model as an on-device fallback. This dual-layer architecture
implements graceful degradation, maintaining functional output even under
limited connectivity conditions.

These modules are sustained by a technological foundation comprising Natural
Language Processing, Large Language Models, machine learning, computer vision,
Firebase Authentication and Firestore for persistent user data, and a
gamification system based on experience points (XP) and achievement badges.
At the pedagogical level, the system instantiates four evidence-based
instructional strategies identified in the literature review: personalized
learning, interactive tutoring, formative feedback, and challenge-based
learning.

The overarching goal of the proposal is to improve academic performance,
intrinsic motivation, and the overall learning experience in Natural Sciences
education, contributing a replicable, low-infrastructure-cost model for
AI-enhanced mobile learning in under-resourced educational contexts
(Miranda et al., 2026).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.2. Limitations

Despite the promising results obtained, the implementation and evaluation of
the proposed system are subject to several limitations that must be
transparently acknowledged, as they affect the generalizability of findings
and the scalability of the system (see Figure X, central column).

Response accuracy. Large language models, including LLaMA 3.3 70B, may
generate responses that are incorrect, hallucinated, or outside the intended
curricular scope. Although the context injection and domain restriction
strategies achieved a scientific accuracy rate of 97% and a domain
restriction effectiveness of 98.9%, a residual error rate persists that may
have a negative instructional impact if undetected by the learner.

Internet connectivity dependency. The core AI functionalities — the chatbot,
the dynamic quiz generator, and the Gemini-based image scanner — require an
active internet connection. This represents a significant structural constraint
in educational contexts with limited or unstable digital infrastructure,
particularly in rural or peripheral regions of developing countries.

Instructional design variability. The educational effectiveness of the system
is not solely determined by its technical performance. It depends critically
on the teacher's capacity and willingness to integrate the tool coherently
within a broader pedagogical design. Without structured teacher training and
institutional support, the system's potential may remain unrealized.

Ethical and privacy concerns. The use of generative AI systems in educational
settings raises unresolved challenges regarding data governance, algorithmic
transparency, and user trust — particularly when the users are minors. The
interaction data stored in cloud services is subject to third-party policies
that may not align with local data protection regulations.

Lack of standardized evaluation framework. The field currently lacks a unified
methodological protocol for measuring the educational impact of AI-powered
chatbot and tutoring systems. This absence limits the comparability of results
across studies and hinders cumulative scientific progress.

Limited validation sample. The pilot study was conducted with 45 students
from a single educational institution in Ecuador over a four-week period.
This restricts the external validity of the findings and precludes causal
inference regarding the observed knowledge gains, given the absence of a
randomized control group.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.3. Future Works

The limitations identified above, together with the broader research gaps
surfaced by the systematic review, motivate a set of concrete directions for
future investigation (see Figure X, right column).

Standardized evaluation framework. A priority for the research community is
the development of unified evaluation metrics and protocols for AI-powered
educational chatbot systems. Such a framework should integrate usability
measures (e.g., System Usability Scale), effect size indicators (e.g.,
Cohen's d), and non-parametric statistical tests (e.g., Mann-Whitney U) to
enable valid and reliable cross-study comparisons. The formalization of
assessment dimensions — covering technical performance, pedagogical validity,
and user experience — would substantially advance the scientific rigor of the
field.

Multi-institution validation. Replicating the study across multiple schools,
educational levels, and socioeconomic contexts is essential to validate the
generalizability of the findings. Future research should incorporate
randomized controlled designs that isolate the specific contribution of
individual AI components to learning outcomes, distinguishing their effects
from general instructional factors.

Advanced and reliable AI models. To address limitations in response accuracy
and domain specificity, future implementations should explore Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG) architectures and fine-tuning strategies applied
to nationally validated curriculum content. These techniques would anchor
model outputs more firmly in verified educational sources, reducing
hallucination rates and improving alignment with learning objectives.

Full offline functionality. Integrating on-device large language models
through edge AI deployment — leveraging compressed model architectures such
as quantized LLMs — would substantially reduce connectivity dependency and
improve equity of access in contexts with limited internet infrastructure.
This direction is particularly relevant for educational systems in developing
regions and for ensuring continuity of learning during connectivity
disruptions.

Adaptive learning pathways. Future versions of the system should implement
intelligent content recommendation engines capable of dynamically adjusting
the sequence, difficulty, and modality of learning activities based on each
student's interaction profile, performance history, and knowledge state.
This would enable more individualized learning trajectories and more precise
formative support aligned with each learner's needs.

Expansion to other curricular subjects. The multi-model AI architecture
developed in this study is not inherently specific to Natural Sciences. Its
extension to other school subjects — including Mathematics, History, and
Language — with adapted curricular content and domain-specific evaluation
criteria would establish a scalable and transferable framework for AI-
enhanced mobile education across the school curriculum.

Taken together, these future directions constitute a research agenda oriented
toward the development of chatbot-based educational systems that are
scalable, adaptive, robust, and pedagogically aligned — capable of meeting
the diverse and evolving needs of learners in a variety of educational
contexts.
